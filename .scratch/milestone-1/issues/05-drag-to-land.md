# Drag-to-land valley and mountain Folds, matched to the target

Status: ready-for-agent
Type: task
Blocked by: 03, 04

One finger on the paper picks up the nearest corner or edge point; dragging shows the paper folding live; letting go is matched against the Step's target Fold (right flap, right direction, line within a forgiving tolerance). A match snaps to the exact Fold; a miss springs back calmly. Pencil works the same as one finger. Symmetric Folds in a Step in any order.

## Comments

Two fingers still move the view, and a second finger arriving lets the paper go without a miss. One finger on the background does nothing. Driven from the `cup-1` fixture; the **Fold / Unfold** button stays (it also unfolds the Fold his finger made) until ticket 07's player replaces it.

- **How it works, since the page has no engine.** Everything comes from the Fold's Recording. The held point is a point on one of the drawn edge segments, followed through the Recording (97 samples, taken once when he picks it up). While he drags, the paper is shown at the progress that puts that point nearest his finger on screen (so it works tilted and turned), keeping the offset between finger and point from the moment of the touch so nothing jumps. On letting go, the Fold matches when the point is put down near where the Recording lands it: within 30% of the point's own journey and never more than 0.15 of the paper (`LANDING_SHARE`, `LANDING_REACH`). That single test is the ticket's three: right flap (a point that doesn't move in this Fold can't match), right direction, and the line (30% of the journey is about 17° of the line's direction or 15% of the journey in its position). A corner is picked up from within 0.12 of the paper; a finger up to 0.06 off the edge still counts as on the paper (`CORNER_REACH`, `EDGE_REACH`). A touch that moves under 10 px is neither a Fold nor a miss. A match folds the rest of the way (up to 900 ms), a miss springs back (up to 600 ms), eased, never looping; the paper can't be picked up again until it has settled.
- **Symmetric Folds in any order.** `FoldGesture` is given every Fold still to make in the Step (`host.folds()`), each as its Recording's paper lying as the paper lies now; the Fold he has hold of is the one that carries the touched point furthest, and `folded(index)` reports which was made. Tested with two opposite corners folded to the centre, either first. The fixture has only one Fold, so this is not yet exercised in the page.

- **Flagged: decisions made here that Dad should confirm.**
  1. *The live fold is the Step's Recording, not a free fold.* The paper under his finger only ever moves along the Step's own Fold, so a wrong line is never shown as a wrong fold: dragging the right corner the wrong way moves the paper only as far as that drag goes toward the right landing, and letting go springs back. Showing the fold he actually made (the crease half way between where the point was and where his finger is) needs the engine in the page, which tickets 04 and 10 rule out, and ADR 0001 rejects unplanned paper states. Tickets 04 and 10 both say the finger drives the Recording, so this follows them, but it does make the gesture closer to "push the flap along its path" than "choose the line".
  2. *The wrong flap doesn't move at all.* Picking up a point that isn't on the Step's flap and dragging it is a miss with nothing to spring back from. Ticket 06's pulse of the target line is then the only feedback.
  3. *Valley and mountain are the same drag.* A drag on a flat screen can't say whether the point goes over or under; the Recording decides, and a mountain Fold's flap goes underneath (tested). "Right direction" in the ticket is therefore read as which way the point travels, not V against M.
  4. *The match is strict for points held near the fold line.* It is tested by where the held point lands, so a point picked up very near the fold line travels little and has to be landed correspondingly precisely (30% of a short journey). Corners are picked up first, which usually avoids this.
  5. The tdd skill asks for the test seams to be agreed with the user first; as a background agent I chose them (the three pure modules above, with `FoldGesture` driven by pixels over a real Recording and a real camera) without that conversation.
- **No disagreement between the spec and DESIGN.md was found for this ticket.** (Ticket 06's "after three misses" against DESIGN.md's "after a few" is 06's to settle.)
- **Known limits.** Near the end of a plain Fold the flap is drawn steeper than the finger suggests (a point 8% short of its landing, seen from above, is a flap still 33° off the table: true of a rigid turn, but it may look as if the paper lags; the snap finishes it). The on-the-paper test and the landing use the table plane, so with the view tilted a touch on a standing flap is read where it meets the table (nothing stands while a Step waits, so this matters only for the landing, which is forgiving). With layers lying exactly on each other, the layer picked up is the one the Fold moves furthest, then the top one: forgiving, not a test of choosing the layer. No palm rejection for the Pencil: a resting palm is a second pointer and lets the paper go. A Fold of every layer that leaves no drawn edge moving (none known) could not be picked up, since only edge points are held.
