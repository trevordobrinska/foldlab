# A real closed sink, recorded and played back like the squash

Status: ready-for-agent
Type: task
Blocked by: 11

## Why

The inside reverse (ticket 10) and the squash (ticket 11) are now real Folds. Dad checked both on the iPad. The closed sink tab is still the hand-made animation from ticket 02, which stretches the paper. It is the last of the three gestures he tries before they're final (ticket 02). It gets the same treatment: the engine knows the exact finished shape, the physics records the Fold once ahead of time, and his finger plays the recording.

A closed sink pushes a closed point down inside the model without opening it out flat. The layers stay wrapped around each other, so the layer order differs from an open sink. Real paper can't make a closed sink without bending a little on the way. The physics may let faces bend during the motion, but not stretch, and they must be flat again at the end.

## What to build

1. **The engine knows the closed sink.** Add `closedSink` to `src/engine/paper.ts`: the closed point above a given line is pushed down inside, along that line, with the layers staying wrapped. The engine gives the finished flat state and layer order, with tests. Reuse the shared cut/perform machinery.
2. **Checked against real origami, not by eye.** Tests compare the engine's result with known results:
   - **Required:** a closed sink of the top (closed) point of a preliminary base. The engine can build one through two squashes (ticket 11). Check the known outline and crease pattern: the point is cut off along the line, and the sunk part's creases swap mountain and valley.
   - Every vertex is flat-foldable (Maekawa and Kawasaki), and Rabbit Ear's layer solver agrees with the engine's layer order.
   - The layer order is a *closed* sink, not an open one. Name the specific orders that tell them apart and test them.
3. **Recorded, then played.** `npm run record` also writes `proto-gestures/recordings/closed-sink.json`. The Closed sink tab plays it; the page still contains no physics. The gesture stays as it is (press and hold until the ring fills, then push the point down; Dad accepted the hold). The ring and arrow line up with the recorded paper.
4. **The physics may improve** for the bending the sink needs, as long as every ticket 10 and 11 test still passes. If either recording's output changes, re-make it.

## Done when (automatic tests, every one passing)

The shared acceptance tests (`src/motion/acceptance.ts`), run on the closed sink recording at at least 50 samples including points halfway between snapshots: no stretching (1%), no passing through, right start and finish with layer order, smooth, earlier Folds hold, spring-back. Plus:

- **A real closed sink:** the engine's result matches the known outline and creases, with closed-sink layer order.
- **Flat-foldable:** Maekawa and Kawasaki at every vertex; Rabbit Ear agrees with the layer order.
- **Symmetric:** the model's mirror symmetry holds throughout the recording.
- **Bends, then flat:** any bending during the motion is within a stated limit, and the finished state is flat.
- **Fast and fresh:** playback under 1 ms per frame, each committed recording matches a fresh one, and no physics in the page.
- `npm run typecheck`, `npm test` and `npm run build` pass. Screenshots at the start, halfway and finished look right. Dad checks it on the iPad.

## Out of scope

- Engine: `closedSink(paper, { line, point })` in `src/engine/paper.ts`. Every layer under his finger with paper beyond the line, all joined there (else `no-flap`: a book's open edge is not a closed point), is a closed point; each layer's tip turns over the line onto that layer's inside, where the inside is away from the model's outside, which is the side the nearest layer shows him (a folded crease turns the outside over with the face's winding, so a tip goes toward `-outside × winding`). Built on the shared cut/perform with `invertInside` (the creases inside the sunk part swap) and the landing rules "tips keep their layers' order among themselves and against the layers that stay"; a tip lies against its own layer by perform's own rule. The preliminary base's nearest-layer lookup had to stop relying on `facesUnder`'s sort, which is not a total order when his finger is on the centre line (the two sides don't overlap).
- Reference result (`paper.test.ts`, "closed sink"), derived from standard origami geometry rather than taken from a diagram: the preliminary base of ticket 11 (closed point B = (0, 0.5), open corners A = (0.5, 1)) sunk along the line through (0.2, 0.5) and (0, 0.7). Each layer's tip lands mirrored across the line, so the point is cut off and the sunk tip reaches B′ = (0.2, 0.7) inside the model: 8 body quadrilaterals and 8 tip triangles, 4 of each on each side of the centre line, exact shapes checked. Crease pattern: the sink line is the square round the centre with corners 0.2 out on the midlines and sides crossing the diagonals 0.1 out, all 8 segments one way (valley here: mountain from the base's outside); inside it the 4 midline tips go V → M, the 2 folded diagonal tips M → V, the 2 flat diagonal tips stay F; outside it nothing changes (creases sorted: FFFF MMMMMM + 14 V). Maekawa and Kawasaki hold at every vertex; Rabbit Ear's `solveFaceOrders`, given only the folded form, finds a single solution equal to ours (56 orders).
- Closed, not open, by layer order (two named tests, on each side of the centre line, layers nearest him first): the only flat-foldable order with every sunk crease swapped is body, tip, tip, body, body, tip, tip, body, where (1) every sunk tip lies against the inside of its own layer (the front sheet's tip just behind it, the back sheet's just in front, and the folded flap's two tips outside its two layers, wrapping them: the model's two interior pockets each hold two tips), and (2) the sunk point keeps its layers in the model's order front to back, as one point turned inside out. Why: every layer at the point reaches the sink line, so nothing may come between a tip and its own layer at that crease, and the tip creases (from the square's corners to B′) run through the interior of every body, so nothing may come between the two tips they join either; with the swapped creases that leaves exactly this order. An open sink opens the point out instead: the folded flap's tip creases keep the body's sign and its tips fold together with a sheet's tip as one unit to one side (two mirror-image arrangements, which is why diagrams say the open sink's inner layers can go either way); its tip of the flap would lie behind the flap, not wrapped round it. Lang's distinction (closed: the layers stay wrapped, locked) and the folding tip "for a closed sink the diagonal inside the square is a mountain, for an open sink a valley" agree with this.

- Physics: **not achieved.** The solver as it is jams: the closed point does not move at all (bars strained 1.7%, facets bent 32°, earlier creases 14–16° off while the driven creases fall 330° behind their targets), and the recording's last snapshot is the engine's finished Paper pressed on, 0.28 away. The cause is geometric, not tuning: all eight tips share the closed point and their eight sink creases lie on one line in the folded model, so each tip's apex would have to swing out of the plane in a different direction while being one vertex; a rigid tip can only swing as a stack (which reverses the tips' order, which the bodies then forbid), so the point can go in only by bending finer than the mesh can (a tip triangle has 12 facets) or by the model opening its mouth as hands do. Tried and measured, none good enough: contacts off (no change: the jam is not the contacts); tip creases undriven or driven late; softer facets; the hand pinning the point and pushing it along the centre line (it then arrives within 0.015 of the end but crumples: bars 7%, facets 170°, layers passing through one another at creases, earlier creases flipped); creases forbidden from folding past flat (no help, reverted); holding only the back sheet so the mouth may open (still jammed). Not tried for lack of time: a prescribed path that opens the model as a cup and inverts the tip through the flat sink square (rigid paper needs the model opened out flat for that, so it would still need bending), or a finer tip mesh.
